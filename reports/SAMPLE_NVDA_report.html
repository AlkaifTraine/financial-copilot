NVIDIA demonstrates exceptional business quality with rapid revenue growth (126% in FY2024, 114% in FY2025) and expanding operating margins (54% in FY2024 to 62% in FY2025), reflecting strong competitive positioning and operational leverage.

What the market prices in

The current share price implies first-year revenue growth of approximately 77% with sustained high growth thereafter, alongside operating margins near or above 60%, reflecting a highly bullish scenario.

What we expect

Our base case assumes more conservative revenue growth of 60% in year one, tapering over time, with terminal operating margins of 48.3% and a terminal growth rate of 2.5%, resulting in a DCF fair value of USD 155.32.

The gap. The market’s valuation assumes growth and margin profiles closer to our bull case (USD 331 fair value), while we see risks to sustaining such elevated levels; this divergence results in a 29% downside to our intrinsic value and supports a SELL recommendation.

Business Overview

NVIDIA’s revenue is dominated by its Compute & Networking segment, driven by AI-focused data center products, with a concentrated customer base that may influence future growth dynamics.

NVIDIA’s business is divided into two main operating segments: Compute & Networking and Graphics. In fiscal year 2026, Compute & Networking generated $193.5 billion in revenue, accounting for approximately 90% of total revenue of $215.9 billion, while Graphics contributed $22.5 billion [1]. This heavy skew towards Compute & Networking indicates that NVIDIA’s core business and profitability are primarily tied to data center and AI-related computing platforms rather than traditional graphics products. The dominance of this segment suggests that NVIDIA’s growth and risk profile are closely linked to demand for accelerated computing and AI infrastructure.

The Compute & Networking segment’s revenue growth is driven by demand for NVIDIA’s Blackwell computing platform and networking products such as NVLink and XDR InfiniBand, which support AI workloads and cloud service providers [3][4]. This focus on AI and data center networking reflects a strategic pivot from legacy graphics to high-performance computing solutions. Given the rapid adoption of AI technologies and cloud infrastructure, this demand is likely sustainable in the medium term, although it depends on continued innovation and customer adoption of NVIDIA’s platforms. Disruptions in AI investment or competitive advances could challenge this growth trajectory.

NVIDIA’s customer base is highly concentrated, with a few direct customers accounting for a significant portion of revenue—one direct customer represented 22% of total revenue in fiscal 2026, primarily within Compute & Networking [5][6]. This concentration implies dependency risks but also indicates strong relationships with major cloud providers and hyperscalers who drive AI infrastructure demand. The sustainability of this revenue concentration depends on maintaining these partnerships and the customers’ continued investment in AI and data center capacity. Any loss or reduction in orders from these key customers could materially impact NVIDIA’s revenue.

Geographically, the majority of NVIDIA’s revenue is attributed to customers headquartered in the United States, which accounted for $149.6 billion or 69% of total revenue in fiscal 2026, with Taiwan and China contributing smaller shares [2][5]. However, a significant portion of Taiwan-based customer revenue is ultimately linked to end customers in the US and Europe, underscoring NVIDIA’s reliance on Western markets for demand. This geographic concentration exposes NVIDIA to geopolitical and trade risks but also aligns with the global centers of AI innovation and cloud infrastructure investment. The persistence of this geographic revenue profile depends on stable trade relations and continued technology leadership in these regions.

  • Compute & Networking segment generated $193.5 billion in FY2026, about 90% of total revenue, showing NVIDIA’s core business is AI and data center focused [1].
  • Revenue growth in Compute & Networking driven by Blackwell platform and networking products, indicating strategic shift towards AI infrastructure [3][4].
  • One direct customer accounted for 22% of total revenue in FY2026, highlighting customer concentration risk but also strong cloud provider partnerships [5][6].
  • 69% of revenue in FY2026 came from US-headquartered customers, reflecting geographic concentration in Western markets with associated geopolitical risks [2][5].

What it means. NVIDIA’s business model is deeply embedded in AI-driven data center computing with concentrated customers and geographies, making sustained innovation and customer retention critical for investment returns.

Financial Performance

NVIDIA's robust revenue growth driven by AI and accelerated computing is tempered by margin compression from product transitions and inventory charges.

NVIDIA's fiscal 2026 revenue surged 65% to $215.9 billion, fueled primarily by a 68% increase in Data Center sales and strong demand for Blackwell architecture across segments, including a 41% rise in Gaming and 70% growth in Professional Visualization [1][2]. This reflects the company's successful capture of accelerated computing and AI-driven market shifts, indicating a structural uplift in demand rather than a cyclical spike. Given the ongoing expansion of AI workloads and platform adoption, this revenue momentum is likely sustainable, although supply constraints in Gaming could moderate near-term growth [2][7].

Gross margin declined by 3.9 percentage points to 71.1% in fiscal 2026, driven by the transition from Hopper HGX to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions and a significant $4.5 billion charge related to H20 excess inventory and purchase obligations [1][2][8]. This margin compression signals the cost of scaling new architectures and managing inventory risks amid rapid product evolution. While the margin impact from inventory charges is a one-time headwind, the structural shift to Blackwell solutions may exert ongoing pressure on gross margins unless cost efficiencies improve. Sustained margin recovery will depend on NVIDIA's ability to optimize production and inventory management as Blackwell ramps.

Operating expenses rose 41% to $23.1 billion, driven by increased compensation costs from employee growth and higher compute and infrastructure expenses [1][2][4][7]. Despite the absolute increase, operating expenses as a percentage of revenue declined from 12.6% to 10.7%, reflecting operational leverage amid strong top-line growth [3][4]. This suggests NVIDIA is effectively scaling its investment in R&D and SG&A to support innovation and market expansion. The sustainability of this leverage hinges on continued revenue growth outpacing expense increases, which appears feasible given the company's growth drivers.

NVIDIA's net cash from operating activities jumped to $102.7 billion, up 60% year-over-year, supporting substantial free cash flow generation despite increased investing and financing outflows [5][6]. This robust cash flow underpins NVIDIA's capacity to fund R&D, capital expenditures, and shareholder returns without compromising financial flexibility. The durability of this cash flow strength depends on maintaining revenue growth and managing working capital, particularly inventory levels, which remain a key risk factor given recent excesses.

  • 65% revenue growth to $215.9 billion driven by AI and Data Center demand indicates structural market expansion.
  • Gross margin fell 3.9 points to 71.1% due to product transition and a $4.5 billion inventory charge, pressuring profitability.
  • Operating expenses increased 41% but declined as a percentage of revenue to 10.7%, showing operational leverage.
  • Net operating cash flow rose 60% to $102.7 billion, enabling strong free cash flow and financial flexibility.
  • Supply constraints in Gaming and inventory management remain near-term risks to margin and revenue sustainability.

What it means. NVIDIA's financial performance confirms strong AI-driven growth but margin pressures and inventory risks require monitoring, making execution on cost control and supply chain critical for sustaining profitability and cash flow.

Growth Drivers

NVIDIA's growth is driven by AI platform demand and accelerated computing, but supply chain and product transition risks could temper sustainability.

NVIDIA's fiscal 2026 revenue surged 65% to $215.9 billion, with Data Center revenue up 68% driven by accelerated computing and AI, and Gaming revenue rising 41% due to Blackwell demand [1]. This indicates that AI and accelerated computing platforms are the primary visible growth engines, reflecting strong market adoption of NVIDIA's latest architectures. However, the sustainability of this growth depends on continued innovation and market acceptance of new AI platforms like Blackwell, which could falter if competitors catch up or if customer infrastructure readiness lags.

The company is navigating complex product transitions across Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, and Automotive segments, with increased cadence of new architectures each year [2][4]. This complexity introduces volatility in revenue due to qualification times, channel inventory adjustments, and potential customer delays in adopting new architectures. While NVIDIA has managed such transitions before, the accelerated pace and product variety heighten risks of supply-demand mismatches and margin pressure, suggesting that growth from new architectures may be uneven and subject to execution risks.

NVIDIA is investing heavily in expanding U.S.-based manufacturing and specialized equipment to support domestic production and meet AI infrastructure demand [2][4][7][8]. This strategic shift aims to strengthen supply chain resilience and reduce dependency on external factors, which is critical given past supply constraints and inventory charges [1][6]. However, the success of this initiative depends on the domestic manufacturing ecosystem's ability to scale timely; delays could constrain supply and limit revenue growth, making this a potential bottleneck for sustaining momentum.

Macroeconomic and geopolitical factors, including tariffs, inflation, and export controls, add uncertainty to NVIDIA's supply chain and cost structure [6][7][8]. These factors could impact product availability, pricing flexibility, and customer demand timing. Although NVIDIA manages these risks actively, the persistent external pressures may continue to cause revenue volatility and margin fluctuations, challenging the durability of growth unless mitigated by supply chain improvements and stable global conditions.

  • 65% revenue growth in fiscal 2026 driven by AI and accelerated computing signals strong demand for NVIDIA's new platforms [1], supporting near-term expansion.
  • Increased frequency of product transitions across segments raises risk of revenue volatility and margin pressure due to supply-demand imbalances [2][4].
  • Investment in U.S.-based manufacturing aims to enhance supply chain resilience but depends on ecosystem scalability, posing a potential growth constraint [2][4][7][8].
  • $4.5 billion inventory charge linked to excess H20 inventory highlights risks from supply-demand mismatches and product transition challenges [1].
  • Macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainties continue to complicate supply chain and cost management, potentially impacting revenue timing and margins [6][7][8].

What it means. NVIDIA's growth prospects hinge on successful execution of AI-driven product transitions and supply chain expansion, but investors should monitor risks from manufacturing scale-up and external uncertainties that could disrupt momentum.

Competitive Position

NVIDIA faces intensifying competition and geopolitical export restrictions that challenge its market share and growth sustainability.

NVIDIA competes in a highly dynamic market with rivals ranging from established semiconductor firms like AMD, Intel, and Broadcom to large cloud providers such as Amazon and Microsoft that develop internal AI hardware solutions [1]. This breadth of competition pressures NVIDIA on multiple fronts including pricing, product performance, and innovation. The company’s reliance on fabless manufacturing and third-party suppliers adds vulnerability to supply constraints and cost premiums, which competitors with in-house fabs may better manage [2][6]. This competitive intensity is unlikely to abate as new entrants and alliances emerge, making NVIDIA’s ability to anticipate customer needs and maintain product quality at competitive prices critical for preserving market share [6].

Geopolitical export controls, especially those restricting sales to China’s data center market, have materially impaired NVIDIA’s competitive position and allowed rivals to expand their ecosystems in this key region [3][4][5]. The complex licensing requirements and uncertain regulatory environment create ongoing risks that could further limit NVIDIA’s access to global markets and increase operational burdens. Unless NVIDIA can develop products compliant with both US and Chinese regulations, this lost market opportunity will persist and continue to erode its global competitiveness [3][4][5].

NVIDIA’s competitive challenges are compounded by long manufacturing lead times and inventory risks, which require advance commitments and premium payments to secure supply [2][6][7]. These factors increase costs and reduce flexibility in responding to rapid shifts in demand or competitor actions. Additionally, some customers possess internal development capabilities that could substitute NVIDIA’s offerings, further intensifying competitive pressure [2]. The company’s ability to manage supply chain resilience and accurately forecast demand will be essential to sustaining its competitive position amid these operational complexities [6][7].

  • NVIDIA faces competition from major semiconductor firms and cloud providers with greater resources, intensifying pricing and innovation pressures [1][2].
  • Export controls have effectively foreclosed NVIDIA from China’s data center market, benefiting competitors and reducing NVIDIA’s global reach [3][4][5].
  • Long manufacturing lead times and supply chain constraints force NVIDIA to pay premiums and make early commitments, increasing costs and operational risk [2][6][7].
  • Customers with internal hardware development capabilities pose a substitution threat, potentially reducing NVIDIA’s market share [2].
  • The evolving regulatory landscape, including potential new export restrictions, adds uncertainty that could further impair NVIDIA’s competitive position [3][4][5].

What it means. NVIDIA’s competitive position is under sustained pressure from geopolitical restrictions and intensifying rivals, making its future growth and margin expansion contingent on navigating regulatory hurdles and supply chain challenges effectively.

Outlook

NVIDIA's aggressive share repurchase and dividend strategy signals strong cash flow confidence but depends on sustained profitability and market conditions.

NVIDIA repurchased 282 million shares for $40.4 billion in fiscal year 2026 and increased its share repurchase authorization by $60 billion without expiration, bringing total available repurchase capacity to $71.2 billion as of August 2025 [1][2][6]. This scale of capital return indicates management's confidence in the company’s free cash flow generation and a strategic focus on enhancing shareholder value through buybacks. However, the continuation of this program depends on maintaining robust cash flow and favorable market conditions, as the company retains discretion to suspend repurchases if operating requirements or investment opportunities dictate.

The company paid $974 million in dividends during fiscal year 2026, nearly doubling the prior year’s payout, with future dividends contingent on board approval and shareholder interests [2][3][6]. This growing dividend commitment reflects a shift toward returning capital directly to shareholders alongside buybacks, signaling maturity in cash generation. Sustainability of dividends will require consistent earnings and cash flow, especially given the company’s emphasis on balancing reinvestment in technology with shareholder returns.

NVIDIA’s free cash flow of $97 billion in fiscal year 2026, with $41 billion returned to shareholders (43% of free cash flow), demonstrates a disciplined capital allocation approach that balances growth investments and shareholder distributions [5]. The company’s explicit strategy to invest in technology and ecosystem development while maintaining substantial capital returns suggests management expects durable growth and profitability. This balance is sustainable only if NVIDIA continues to capitalize on its market position and manages supply chain and operational risks effectively.

Guidance for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 projects $78 billion in revenue, driven mainly by Data Center growth, with gross margins expected in the mid-70% range [5]. The exclusion of Data Center compute revenue from China in the outlook signals cautiousness around geopolitical risks that could impact growth. Maintaining these margins and revenue growth is critical to sustaining the cash flow that underpins the capital return programs. Any disruption in Data Center demand or margin compression would challenge the current capital allocation strategy.

  • 282 million shares repurchased for $40.4 billion in FY2026 shows strong cash flow confidence.
  • Additional $60 billion buyback authorization without expiration signals long-term commitment to capital return.
  • $974 million dividends paid in FY2026 nearly double prior year, indicating increased shareholder income focus.
  • 43% of $97 billion free cash flow returned to shareholders balances growth investment and returns.
  • $78 billion Q1 revenue guidance driven by Data Center growth but excludes China, reflecting geopolitical caution.
  • Mid-70% gross margin guidance essential to sustaining cash flow for buybacks and dividends.

What it means. NVIDIA’s robust capital return program, supported by strong free cash flow and disciplined investment, enhances shareholder value but hinges on sustained Data Center growth and margin stability amid geopolitical uncertainties.

Competitive Moat

Moat · Narrow

NVIDIA's moat is primarily driven by its CUDA ecosystem and broad platform presence, which provide some switching costs and network effects; however, intense competition and technological challenges limit the durability of these advantages, making the assumed terminal margin of 48.3% plausible but not defensible at the much higher market-implied margin of 74%.

Sources of advantage

  • CUDA software ecosystem widely adopted across cloud providers, OEMs, edge devices, and emerging AI-driven telecommunications platforms, creating switching costs and developer lock-in
  • Broad customer base across diverse AI and accelerated computing applications including robotics, autonomous vehicles, and scientific computing, which supports platform stickiness
  • Proprietary specialized hardware (Tensor cores) integrated with flexible CUDA programming model, enabling performance differentiation
  • Strong presence in multiple computing layers (GPUs, DPUs, SoCs) and partnerships with cloud and device makers, reinforcing ecosystem effects

Threats to the moat

  • Increasing competition from established semiconductor companies (AMD, Intel) and large cloud providers (Amazon, Microsoft, Alibaba) developing internal AI hardware solutions
  • Emergence of new competitors or alliances that may offer lower-priced or higher-performance alternatives
  • Potential technological shifts that could reduce the relevance of CUDA or NVIDIA’s specialized hardware
  • Competitors with greater marketing, financial, and manufacturing resources adapting faster to customer or technology changes
