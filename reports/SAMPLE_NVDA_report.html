Business quality · Exceptional Stock · SELL

NVIDIA demonstrates exceptional business quality with rapid revenue growth (126% in FY2024, 114% in FY2025), expanding operating margins (projected 60%+ by FY2026), and strong market leadership in AI infrastructure and data center solutions.

What the market prices in

The current share price implies an aggressive first-year revenue growth of approximately 81% with sustained high growth thereafter, alongside operating margins expanding to mid-70% gross margins and continued margin expansion, reflecting Wall Street’s consensus target of USD 300.

What we expect

We forecast a more moderate first-year revenue growth of 60%, with operating margins stabilizing around 48.3% in the terminal period and a terminal growth rate of 2.5%, resulting in a DCF fair value of USD 145.52 and a probability-weighted fair value of USD 165.23.

The gap. The divergence arises because the market prices in near-perfect execution and sustained hyper-growth that we view as optimistic given the deceleration in recent revenue growth and margin normalization risks; this gap results in a 34% downside to our intrinsic value, justifying a SELL stance.

Why SELL
  1. 1. Our reverse DCF shows the market expects 81% revenue growth in year one, which exceeds our 60% forecast, indicating over-optimism.
  2. 2. Operating margins are expected by the market to reach mid-70% gross margins, while we model a more conservative terminal operating margin of 48.3%.
  3. 3. Recent revenue growth has been volatile, with FY2023 flat growth and a sharp rebound in FY2024 and FY2025, suggesting sustainability risks.
  4. 4. The company’s high operating expense growth (expected ~38% in FY2026) could pressure margins despite gross margin expansion.
  5. 5. Our bear case scenario values the stock at USD 66, reflecting meaningful downside risk if growth or margin assumptions disappoint.
Biggest catalyst

Quarterly earnings releases showing revenue growth or margin expansion materially above or below our base case assumptions, which would validate or challenge the current valuation gap.

Biggest risk

The primary risk to our SELL recommendation is that NVIDIA significantly outperforms our growth and margin assumptions, sustaining hyper-growth and margin expansion beyond our base case, which would justify higher valuations.

Reading the valuation. Our intrinsic DCF values NVDA at USD 145.52, against a market price of USD 219.31 and a Wall Street consensus target of USD 300.00 (the market's view, which our thesis weighs against). Holding every other assumption fixed, today's price implies first-year revenue growth of about 81.5%, decaying toward the terminal rate. Judge that figure against the reported history below rather than treating the rating as a forecast.

Business Overview

NVIDIA's revenue is heavily driven by its Compute & Networking segment, anchored in AI and accelerated computing, with concentrated customer exposure posing risks and opportunities.

NVIDIA's business is divided into two main operating segments: Compute & Networking and Graphics, with Compute & Networking generating $193.5 billion or roughly 90% of total revenue in fiscal 2026, compared to $22.5 billion from Graphics [2]. This concentration indicates NVIDIA's primary value creation stems from its data center and networking products, particularly those supporting AI workloads, rather than traditional graphics. The dominance of Compute & Networking is likely sustainable given the ongoing platform shifts towards accelerated computing and AI, but any disruption in data center demand or technology shifts away from NVIDIA's platforms could materially impact revenue concentration and growth.

The company’s revenue growth is driven by major platform shifts—accelerated computing and AI—with Data Center revenue up 68% year-over-year and Gaming revenue increasing 41%, fueled by demand for the Blackwell architecture [3]. This suggests NVIDIA’s product portfolio is effectively aligned with high-growth technology trends, enabling rapid scaling. However, supply constraints noted for Gaming in early fiscal 2027 could temporarily limit this growth, and sustained innovation is required to maintain leadership in these fast-evolving markets.

NVIDIA’s revenue is geographically concentrated, with 69% of fiscal 2026 revenue coming from U.S.-headquartered customers, and a significant portion of Taiwan-based Data Center revenue ultimately serving U.S. and European end customers [1][5]. This geographic concentration reflects NVIDIA’s reliance on a limited number of large direct customers, especially in Compute & Networking, where one customer accounted for 22% of total revenue and another for 14% in fiscal 2026 [5][6]. While this concentration can drive scale and operational efficiency, it also introduces customer-specific risk that could affect revenue stability if these relationships weaken or if geopolitical factors disrupt supply chains.

The company’s customer base includes direct customers such as AIBs, OEMs, and hyperscalers, as well as indirect customers like cloud service providers and enterprises, with revenue heavily concentrated among a few large customers [6]. This concentration underscores NVIDIA’s critical role in the AI and cloud infrastructure ecosystem but also means that shifts in purchasing patterns by a small number of customers could disproportionately affect NVIDIA’s top line. The sustainability of this model depends on continued adoption of NVIDIA’s platforms by these key customers and the company’s ability to diversify its customer base over time.

  • Compute & Networking segment generated $193.5 billion, about 90% of total revenue, highlighting NVIDIA’s reliance on data center and AI workloads [2].
  • Data Center revenue grew 68% year-over-year, driven by accelerated computing and AI platform shifts, signaling strong alignment with growth markets [3].
  • U.S.-headquartered customers accounted for 69% of revenue in fiscal 2026, indicating geographic and customer concentration risk [1][5].
  • Two direct customers represented 36% of total revenue in fiscal 2026, emphasizing dependency on a limited customer base [5][6].
  • Graphics segment revenue grew 41% driven by Blackwell architecture, but remains a smaller portion of total revenue at $22.5 billion [2][3].

What it means. NVIDIA’s business model, centered on AI-driven Compute & Networking products with concentrated customer exposure, offers strong growth potential but requires monitoring of customer and supply risks for sustained investment returns.

Financial Performance

NVIDIA's robust revenue growth driven by AI and accelerated computing is tempered by margin compression and rising expenses, challenging sustainability.

NVIDIA's revenue surged 65% to $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026, primarily fueled by Data Center growth of 68% and strong demand for Blackwell architecture in Gaming and Professional Visualization segments [1][4]. This revenue expansion reflects the company's successful capture of accelerated computing and AI market shifts, indicating strong market positioning in high-growth areas. However, sustaining this growth depends on continued innovation and overcoming supply constraints, particularly in Gaming where supply headwinds are expected to persist into fiscal 2027 [4].

Gross margin declined by 3.9 percentage points to 71.1% due to the transition from Hopper HGX to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions and a significant $4.5 billion charge related to excess inventory and purchase obligations [1][4]. This margin compression signals pressure from evolving product mix and inventory management challenges, which could persist if demand forecasting and supply chain optimization do not improve. The margin impact from the H20 inventory charge is a one-time event, but the structural shift to lower-margin Blackwell solutions may continue to weigh on profitability unless offset by scale or cost efficiencies.

Operating expenses rose 41% to $23.1 billion, driven by increased compensation, employee growth, and higher compute and infrastructure costs [1][4][6]. While this investment supports innovation and capacity expansion critical to maintaining competitive advantage, the faster growth in expenses relative to revenue growth suggests margin pressure and the need for disciplined cost management. The sustainability of operating leverage gains will depend on NVIDIA's ability to balance growth investments with operational efficiency as competitive and technological dynamics evolve.

NVIDIA's net income increased 65% to $120.1 billion, with diluted EPS up 67% to $4.90, reflecting strong top-line growth and operational leverage despite margin headwinds [1][3]. Cash flow from operations also grew substantially, supporting ongoing R&D and capital expenditures [2]. However, the decline in gross margin and rising expenses caution that profit expansion may moderate if supply constraints and product mix shifts persist. The company's financial strength provides flexibility, but maintaining earnings momentum requires managing these margin and cost challenges effectively.

  • Revenue grew 65% to $215.9 billion, driven by 68% Data Center growth, showing strong demand for AI and accelerated computing solutions [1][4].
  • Gross margin fell 3.9 points to 71.1%, impacted by product transition and a $4.5 billion inventory charge, indicating margin pressure from evolving business mix [1][4].
  • Operating expenses increased 41% to $23.1 billion due to higher compensation and infrastructure costs, highlighting rising investment costs amid growth [1][6].
  • Net income rose 65% to $120.1 billion with EPS up 67% to $4.90, reflecting operational leverage despite margin compression [1][3].
  • Free cash flow reached $96.6 billion, supporting continued R&D and capital spending critical for sustaining competitive positioning [2].

What it means. NVIDIA's impressive revenue and earnings growth driven by AI and accelerated computing is challenged by margin compression and rising expenses, requiring careful cost control and supply management to sustain profitability and justify valuation.

Growth Drivers

NVIDIA's revenue growth is driven by rapid AI-driven product transitions and expanding data center offerings, but supply chain and transition complexities pose ongoing risks.

NVIDIA's fiscal 2026 revenue surged 65%, with Data Center revenue up 68% and Gaming up 41%, primarily fueled by accelerated computing and AI platform shifts like Blackwell, indicating strong market demand for AI-optimized products [2]. This growth confirms the company's successful capture of AI-driven computing needs, but the sustainability depends on continued innovation and market adoption of new architectures.

The company is executing a strategy of annual new Data Center computing solutions and more diverse offerings, reflecting a faster product and architecture cadence to meet AI opportunities [1][3][7]. While this approach supports ongoing revenue expansion, it also increases complexity in supply-demand management and heightens risks of revenue volatility and inventory costs due to product transitions and qualification delays. These operational challenges could persist unless NVIDIA improves supply chain agility and transition execution.

NVIDIA's investment in U.S.-based manufacturing aims to scale production capacity domestically, but the ability to meet demand hinges on the broader ecosystem's ramp-up speed [1][3][7]. Delays or shortfalls in scaling could constrain revenue growth despite strong demand. This bottleneck risk is structural and will remain until the domestic manufacturing infrastructure matures sufficiently to support NVIDIA's volume requirements.

The company faces demand forecasting challenges as customers may delay adopting new architectures or reduce orders ahead of transitions, causing revenue timing volatility and inventory write-downs, exemplified by the Blackwell low-yielding material impacting margins [1][3][7][8]. These dynamics suggest that while AI-driven demand growth is robust, revenue and margin sustainability require better alignment of product launches with customer readiness and supply chain responsiveness.

  • 65% fiscal 2026 revenue growth driven by AI and accelerated computing confirms strong market traction but depends on continued innovation [2].
  • Annual new Data Center solutions increase product variety but magnify supply-demand management complexity and revenue volatility [1][3][7].
  • U.S.-based manufacturing expansion is critical but constrained by ecosystem capacity, risking supply shortfalls [1][3][7].
  • Product transition complexities cause inventory provisions and margin pressure, highlighting operational risks during rapid innovation cycles [1][3][8].
  • Customer delays in adopting new architectures create timing risks for revenue, requiring improved coordination across the value chain [1][3][7].

What it means. NVIDIA's growth is anchored in AI-driven product innovation and expanding data center offerings, but investors should monitor execution risks from supply chain scaling and product transition complexities that could affect near-term revenue and margins.

Competitive Position

NVIDIA faces intensifying competition from well-resourced incumbents and new entrants, with supply chain and regulatory challenges threatening its market share sustainability.

NVIDIA competes against a broad spectrum of established semiconductor firms such as AMD, Intel, and Broadcom, as well as large cloud providers like Amazon and Microsoft that develop internal AI hardware solutions [1]. This diverse competitive set pressures NVIDIA on multiple fronts including pricing, performance, and feature innovation. The presence of competitors with greater manufacturing capabilities and financial resources suggests NVIDIA must continuously innovate and invest to maintain its market position. This competitive intensity is likely to persist given rapid technological evolution and the entry of new players, implying NVIDIA’s advantage depends on sustained R&D and ecosystem partnerships.

Export controls and geopolitical restrictions have materially impaired NVIDIA’s competitive position, exemplified by a $4.5 billion charge related to diminished demand for the H20 product in China [3][7]. These regulatory barriers not only reduce NVIDIA’s addressable market but also increase operational complexity and costs, potentially driving customers toward local competitors. Unless these export constraints are eased or alternative markets expanded, NVIDIA’s competitive edge in key regions will remain constrained, limiting growth and allowing rivals to capture market share.

NVIDIA’s fabless manufacturing model leverages leading foundries like TSMC and Samsung, enabling focus on design and innovation while avoiding capital-intensive fabrication [4]. However, long lead times and supply chain concentration in Asia expose NVIDIA to capacity risks and cost premiums, especially amid semiconductor shortages [2][6]. Efforts to diversify manufacturing into the U.S. and Latin America may enhance supply chain resilience, but the ability to scale production rapidly depends on external ecosystem capacity. Persistent supply constraints could erode NVIDIA’s ability to meet demand and compete effectively on price and availability.

The rise of open-source AI models and internal hardware development by cloud providers introduces a new dimension of competitive risk [7]. If these models and platforms gain traction on rival hardware, NVIDIA’s product demand could weaken. This trend challenges NVIDIA’s traditional reliance on proprietary technology leadership and underscores the need for strategic investments in AI ecosystems and partnerships to sustain demand. The durability of NVIDIA’s competitive position will hinge on its ability to integrate with and capitalize on emerging AI software trends.

  • NVIDIA’s competitors include AMD, Intel, and cloud giants like Amazon and Microsoft, indicating multi-faceted competitive pressure across hardware and software domains [1].
  • A $4.5 billion inventory charge linked to U.S. export restrictions on the H20 product reveals significant regulatory risks impacting NVIDIA’s market access in China [7].
  • Long manufacturing lead times exceeding 12 months and supply chain concentration in Asia create vulnerability to capacity shortages and cost inflation [2][6].
  • Expansion of manufacturing partnerships into the U.S. and Latin America aims to mitigate supply risks but depends on regional ecosystem readiness [4][7].
  • Open-source AI models threaten to reduce demand for NVIDIA’s proprietary hardware if deployed on competitor platforms, challenging its market dominance [7].
  • Inventory provisions negatively impacted gross margin by 2.6% in fiscal 2026, reflecting the financial consequences of competitive and supply challenges [8].

What it means. NVIDIA’s competitive position is under sustained pressure from entrenched rivals, regulatory constraints, and supply chain risks, requiring vigilant innovation and strategic supply diversification to protect long-term market leadership.

Outlook

NVIDIA's aggressive share repurchase and disciplined capital allocation signal confidence in sustained cash flow and shareholder value creation amid growth investments.

NVIDIA repurchased 282 million shares for $40.4 billion in fiscal year 2026 and secured an additional $60 billion share repurchase authorization without expiration, increasing total available repurchase capacity to $71.2 billion by August 2025 [1][3][6]. This scale of buybacks indicates management's strong conviction in the company's intrinsic value and cash generation capabilities. The open-ended nature of the authorization suggests a strategic emphasis on capital return flexibility, which can persist as long as free cash flow remains robust and market conditions are favorable. However, any significant deterioration in cash flow or a shift in investment priorities could prompt a suspension.

In fiscal year 2026, NVIDIA returned 43% of its free cash flow to shareholders through $41 billion in share repurchases and dividends, with dividends totaling $974 million for the year [2][3]. This balance between returning capital and reinvesting in technology and ecosystem development reflects a disciplined approach to capital allocation aimed at sustaining long-term growth while rewarding shareholders. The sustainability of this payout ratio depends on continued strong free cash flow generation, which management projects through ongoing investments and market expansion. A material decline in revenue growth or margin compression could challenge this balance.

Management's guidance for Q1 fiscal 2027 projects revenue of $78 billion, plus or minus 2%, driven primarily by Data Center growth, with gross margins expected in the mid-70% range [2]. This outlook, excluding Data Center compute revenue from China, signals cautious optimism grounded in current geopolitical realities and supply chain strengths. The margin stability suggests operational efficiency and pricing power are intact. The persistence of these metrics hinges on maintaining leadership in advanced architectures and navigating geopolitical constraints without significant disruption.

The inclusion of stock-based compensation in non-GAAP results starting Q1 fiscal 2027 acknowledges the importance of talent retention in sustaining innovation and competitive advantage [2]. This transparency may slightly affect non-GAAP profitability metrics but underscores a strategic investment in human capital. The ability to attract and retain world-class talent is critical for sustaining NVIDIA's technological edge and growth trajectory. Any failure to do so could impair innovation and competitive positioning.

  • 282 million shares repurchased for $40.4 billion in fiscal 2026 indicates strong confidence in intrinsic value and cash flow [3].
  • Additional $60 billion share repurchase authorization without expiration provides flexible capital return capacity [1][6].
  • Returned 43% of free cash flow to shareholders via $41 billion in buybacks and dividends, balancing growth and shareholder returns [2].
  • Q1 fiscal 2027 revenue guidance of $78 billion, driven by Data Center, reflects cautious optimism amid geopolitical constraints [2].
  • Gross margins expected in mid-70% range signal operational efficiency and pricing power sustainability [2].
  • Inclusion of stock-based compensation in non-GAAP results highlights investment in talent critical for long-term innovation [2].

What it means. NVIDIA's capital allocation strategy, supported by strong cash flow and disciplined investment, underpins a sustainable growth and shareholder return profile, reinforcing its investment appeal.

Competitive Moat

Moat · Narrow

NVIDIA's moat is primarily based on its proprietary CUDA software ecosystem and NVLink hardware innovations, which provide significant performance-per-watt advantages critical for AI inference workloads. However, while these advantages support a high operating margin, the implied market margin of 80.6% is unrealistic; our assumed terminal margin of 48.3% better reflects the sustainable advantage given competitive pressures and technological challenges.

Sources of advantage

  • Proprietary CUDA software stack enabling optimized AI inference workloads and developer lock-in
  • NVLink 72 interconnect technology delivering 50x generational performance per watt improvements
  • Strong integration of hardware and software enabling superior performance per dollar and watt
  • Deep relationships with hyperscalers and cloud service providers who value performance-per-watt

Threats to the moat

  • Emerging AI accelerators from competitors (e.g., AMD, Intel, Google TPU) narrowing performance gaps
  • Potential open-source or alternative software stacks reducing CUDA lock-in over time
  • Technological challenges in sustaining generational leaps in performance per watt beyond Moore's Law
  • Increasing pricing pressure as cloud providers seek cost efficiencies amid growing compute demand
