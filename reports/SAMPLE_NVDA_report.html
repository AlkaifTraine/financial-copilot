Business Overview

NVIDIA generates revenue primarily from Compute & Networking and Graphics segments, serving direct and indirect customers globally with AI-driven computing platforms.

NVIDIA's revenue is concentrated in two operating segments: Compute & Networking and Graphics. In fiscal year 2026, Compute & Networking accounted for $193.5 billion of the total $215.9 billion revenue, representing approximately 90%, while Graphics contributed $22.5 billion or about 10% [5]. The Compute & Networking segment's growth is driven by accelerated computing and AI platform shifts, with Data Center computing revenue increasing 59% year-over-year due to demand for the Blackwell platform, and Data Center networking revenue surging 142% fueled by NVLink compute fabric and Ethernet/InfiniBand adoption [2]. Graphics revenue growth is also attributed to sales of the Blackwell architecture [2][6][7][8].

NVIDIA sells primarily to direct customers, including add-in board manufacturers, distributors, ODMs, OEMs, cloud service providers (CSPs), AI model makers, and system integrators, who may further sell to indirect customers such as CSPs, Neocloud builders, enterprises, and public sector entities [1][2][6][7][8]. Revenue concentration is significant, with one direct customer representing 22% and another 14% of total revenue in fiscal 2026, both mainly within Compute & Networking [1][2][6][7]. Indirect customers also contribute materially, with some estimated to represent 10% or more of total revenue, primarily purchasing through direct customers [2][6][7][8].

Geographically, NVIDIA's revenue is predominantly from U.S.-headquartered customers, accounting for $149.6 billion in fiscal 2026, or roughly 69% of total revenue, followed by Taiwan at $42.3 billion and China (including Hong Kong) at $19.7 billion [3]. The company recently shifted to recognizing revenue based on customer headquarters location to better reflect geographic revenue profiles [1].

Gross margin declined to 71.1% in fiscal 2026 from 75.0% in fiscal 2025, impacted by a $4.5 billion charge related to H20 excess inventory and purchase obligations, reflecting a transition from Hopper HGX systems to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions [4]. Despite this, operating income increased year-over-year, with Compute & Networking segment operating income rising to $130.1 billion and Graphics to $9.2 billion in fiscal 2026 [5].

Financial Performance

NVIDIA's FY2026 revenue surged 65% driven by AI and accelerated computing, while margins compressed due to product mix and inventory charges.

NVIDIA reported fiscal year 2026 revenue of $215.9 billion, a 65% increase from $130.5 billion in fiscal 2025, fueled primarily by accelerated computing and AI platform shifts. Data Center revenue rose 68%, propelled by Blackwell architecture adoption and new product launches like DGX Spark, while Gaming revenue grew 41% on strong Blackwell demand despite anticipated supply constraints in FY2027. Professional Visualization and Automotive segments expanded 70% and 39%, respectively, reflecting robust demand for AI solutions and self-driving platforms [1][3][7].

Gross margin declined 3.9 percentage points to 71.1% from 75.0% in FY2025, attributable to the transition from Hopper HGX systems to Blackwell full-scale datacenter solutions and a significant $4.5 billion charge related to H20 excess inventory and purchase obligations. Inventory provisions totaled $7.2 billion in FY2026, substantially higher than $3.7 billion in FY2025, negatively impacting gross margin by 2.6%. This inventory charge, combined with the product mix shift, explains the margin contraction despite strong revenue growth [1][3][8].

Operating expenses increased 41% to $23.1 billion, driven by higher compensation and benefits costs due to employee growth, as well as increased compute and infrastructure expenses supporting new product development. Research and development expenses rose 43% to $18.5 billion, while sales, general and administrative expenses increased 31% to $4.6 billion, reflecting investments in engineering and go-to-market capabilities [1][5][7].

Operating income grew 60% to $130.4 billion, and net income rose 65% to $120.1 billion, with net income per diluted share increasing 67% to $4.90. Despite margin pressures, strong revenue growth and disciplined expense management drove substantial profitability gains. Operating cash flow surged 60% to $102.7 billion, supporting capital expenditures of $6.0 billion and free cash flow of $96.6 billion, underscoring robust cash generation capacity [1][6].

  • Operating expenses rose 41% to $23.1 billion, with R&D up 43% to $18.5 billion and SG&A up 31% to $4.6 billion [1][5].

Growth Drivers

NVIDIA's revenue growth is driven by AI platform Blackwell, expanding AI compute demand, and strategic supply chain investments, with visible gains in data center and automotive segments.

Fiscal year 2026 revenue surged 65% to $215.9 billion, propelled by accelerated computing and AI platform shifts, notably Blackwell, which drove 41% gaming revenue growth and 70% professional visualization growth, evidencing strong market adoption of new AI architectures and DGX Spark systems. Data Center revenue rose 68%, reflecting the centrality of AI workloads, while automotive revenue increased 39%, supported by self-driving platform adoption and the introduction of Alpamayo, an open AI portfolio for autonomous vehicles, signaling expansion into physical AI and robotics markets with partners like Mercedes-Benz and Boston Dynamics [1][2].

Management highlights the exponential growth in AI compute demand across training and inference, with Blackwell Ultra and Vera Rubin architectures delivering significant cost and performance advantages, underpinning enterprise adoption of AI agents and foundation models. The robotaxi market, with fleets scaling from thousands to millions of vehicles, is poised to generate hundreds of billions in revenue, driving orders of magnitude increases in compute requirements and reinforcing NVIDIA’s platform dominance [2][4][5][6].

Visible growth drivers include the ramp of Blackwell-based products, robust data center demand, and automotive platform expansion. Prospective drivers encompass the scaling of AI ecosystems globally, enterprise AI agent adoption, and industrial physical AI applications via partnerships with Dassault Systèmes, Siemens, and Synopsys, which extend NVIDIA’s AI infrastructure into digital twins and simulation, broadening addressable markets beyond traditional computing [2][4].

Supply chain and manufacturing capacity enhancements, particularly increased U.S.-based production and investments in specialized equipment, aim to mitigate supply constraints and support sustained AI infrastructure demand. Despite near-term gaming supply headwinds, strategic inventory and capacity commitments reflect management’s confidence in long-term growth opportunities and operational resilience amid macroeconomic uncertainties [1][2][7][8].

  • Fiscal 2026 revenue grew 65% to $215.9 billion, led by 68% Data Center and 41% Gaming growth driven by Blackwell [1].
  • Automotive revenue rose 39% in fiscal 2026, with $604 million in Q4 driven by self-driving platforms and Alpamayo AI portfolio [1][2].
  • Blackwell Ultra and Vera Rubin architectures deliver order-of-magnitude cost reductions and performance gains in AI inference [5][6].
  • Robotaxi fleets scaling to millions of vehicles forecast hundreds of billions in revenue, driving exponential compute demand [2].
  • Strategic U.S. manufacturing investments aim to strengthen supply chain and meet AI infrastructure demand [7][8].

Competitive Position

NVIDIA faces intensifying competition from established semiconductor firms, cloud providers, and geopolitical export controls that challenge its market share and supply chain resilience.

NVIDIA competes across multiple domains including GPUs, CPUs, DPUs, SoCs, and networking products against formidable rivals such as AMD, Intel, Huawei, and large cloud service companies like Amazon, Microsoft, and Alibaba. These competitors often possess greater financial, manufacturing, and marketing resources, enabling them to adapt more rapidly to technological shifts and customer demands, thereby intensifying competitive pressure on NVIDIA's pricing and market share [1][2]. Additionally, some customers develop internal solutions that could replace NVIDIA’s offerings, further fragmenting demand and increasing competitive risk [2].

Export controls and geopolitical factors have materially harmed NVIDIA’s competitive position, particularly in the China data center market where the company is effectively foreclosed due to U.S. and Chinese government restrictions. This has allowed competitors based in China, Europe, and Israel to expand their ecosystems and challenge NVIDIA globally. The complexity and uncertainty of export licensing, including recent U.S. measures like the rescinded AI Diffusion IFR and the pending GAIN AI Act, add operational burdens and risk loss of market opportunities [3][4].

Supply chain constraints and long manufacturing lead times, sometimes exceeding twelve months, exacerbate NVIDIA’s competitive challenges. The company must commit to non-cancellable purchase orders and pay premiums to secure capacity, which increases costs and risks inventory obsolescence amid fluctuating demand. Mismatches between supply and demand have previously resulted in excess inventory write-downs impacting gross margins by 2.6% in fiscal 2026, and could recur if demand forecasts prove inaccurate or if customers shift to competitors [5][7].

NVIDIA’s strategic response includes increasing U.S.-based manufacturing to enhance supply chain resiliency and meet AI infrastructure demand. However, this transition depends on the domestic ecosystem’s capacity to scale production timely. Macroeconomic factors such as tariffs, inflation, and geopolitical developments continue to complicate supply chain management and pricing strategies, potentially constraining NVIDIA’s ability to compete effectively on cost and delivery [6].

  • NVIDIA’s competitors include AMD, Intel, Huawei, Amazon, Microsoft, and Alibaba across GPUs, CPUs, SoCs, and networking products [1].
  • Export controls have effectively foreclosed NVIDIA from China’s data center market, benefiting competitors in China, Europe, and Israel [3][4].
  • Inventory provisions related to excess or obsolete stock negatively impacted gross margin by 2.6% in fiscal 2026 [5].
  • Manufacturing lead times can exceed 12 months, requiring non-cancellable orders and premium payments to secure capacity [5][7].
  • Fiscal 2026 revenue grew 65%, driven by AI and accelerated computing, but gross margin declined due to inventory charges and product transitions [8].
  • U.S.-based manufacturing investments aim to improve supply chain resiliency but depend on domestic production ramp-up capabilities [6].

Outlook

NVIDIA plans disciplined capital returns with $71.2 billion repurchase authorization and sustained dividends amid strong fiscal 2026 cash flow generation.

NVIDIA's Board of Directors authorized an incremental $60.0 billion share repurchase program on August 26, 2025, raising total repurchase capacity to $71.2 billion as of that date, with no expiration, reflecting management's commitment to returning capital to shareholders while maintaining flexibility to suspend repurchases at its discretion. The company executed substantial buybacks throughout fiscal 2026, repurchasing 282 million shares for $40.4 billion, evidencing aggressive capital deployment to offset dilution and enhance shareholder value amid robust liquidity conditions [1][2][6].

Dividend payments totaled $974 million in fiscal 2026, with quarterly distributions consistently approved by the Board based on shareholder interest alignment. The dividend policy remains contingent on ongoing Board evaluation, indicating a balanced approach between returning cash and preserving capital for strategic investments [1][2][3][4][6].

Fiscal 2026 free cash flow generation was exceptionally strong at $97 billion, enabling NVIDIA to return $41 billion (43% of free cash flow) to shareholders through dividends and repurchases. This robust cash flow underpins management's outlook and capital allocation strategy, which prioritizes disciplined investment in technology and ecosystem development to sustain long-term growth and shareholder returns superior to peers [5].

For fiscal 2027 first quarter guidance, NVIDIA projects revenue of $78 billion ±2%, driven primarily by Data Center growth, while maintaining gross margins in the mid-70% range. The outlook excludes Data Center compute revenue from China, reflecting cautious geopolitical considerations. Stock-based compensation will now be included in non-GAAP results, underscoring the importance of talent retention in sustaining competitive advantage [5].
